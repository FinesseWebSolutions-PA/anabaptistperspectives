Episode 02      |    Conrad Grebel, Felix Manz, George Blaurock

The Choice to Believe

Anabaptists Defied Zurich’s City Council and Baptized Each Other. The Church Struck Back

48 min

1519 – 1527

Zurich, Switzerland; Vienna, Austria; Zollikon, Switzerland

About This Episode

The year is 1525. The place is Zurich, Switzerland, just up the hill from the Limmat River. A small group of young people, including Conrad Grebel, George Blaurock, and Felix Manz, meet secretly in the upstairs room of a house to make a monumental and illegal decision—so illegal that Felix Manz would die for it in that same Limmat River, within earshot of where they met.
Join Dean Taylor, Stephen Russell, and Reagan Schrock as they walk these same streets and lead us through the story. Discover how incredibly personal and risky their decision was to be rebaptized, and how they went from being esteemed students of Zwingli, to outcasts on the run.

Anabaptist Origins is a 10-part documentary series produced by Anabaptist Perspectives that tells the story of the first hundred years of the Anabaptist movement.

    37:30  |  Where the Word Anabaptist Comes From

    Where does the term Anabaptist come into the story? What does that word even mean? Who came up with it?

By using this term, when the reformers used it, they were invoking ancient imperial laws that spelled out that it was against the law to do any kind of rebaptizing. And so they were using these to bring that charge against them.

    These kinds of charges go way back. Emperors such as Justinian I and Theodosius I made these laws about Christianity, combining the church and the state in a very destructive way, in my opinion.

    Exactly. And so these reformers weren’t breaking from the past with this whole concept of church and state and the empire all together. They were channeling it. So here is this term which has now followed us to this day, but it was a loaded word in their day.
