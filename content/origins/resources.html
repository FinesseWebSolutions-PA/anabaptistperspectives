1415

Jan Hus is burned at the stake

A century before the Reformation, a Czech priest is executed for challenging church authority — one of the first sparks.

c. 1450

Gutenberg’s printing press

Movable type makes books cheap and ideas fast and becomes an indispensable tool for the Reformation and the early Anabaptists.

1517

The Reformation begins

Martin Luther’s protest opens a fault line across Europe that the Anabaptists will push further than anyone expects.

1519

Zwingli begins the Swiss Reformation

As people’s priest at the Grossmünster Church in Zurich, Zwingli preaches through the New Testament and draws a circle of eager students.

1525

The first adult baptism

In Zurich, a small group baptizes one another by conviction rather than infancy. Anabaptism is born — and made illegal almost immediately.

1527

The Schleitheim Confession

Seven articles set down what these communities believe. Their author, Michael Sattler, is executed within months.

1534 - 1535

The Münster disaster

A radical faction seizes a German city and tries to force the kingdom of God by the sword. It ends in slaughter. The wider movement spends generations living down what a few men did in its name. 

1536

Menno Simons takes up the work

A former priest gives the movement a steady hand — and, eventually, his name.

1569

Dirk Willems turns back

Fleeing across thin ice, Dirk Willems hears his pursuer break through. He turns back to save the man, is recaptured, and is burned. The story becomes the movement’s most enduring picture of enemy love. 

1632

The Dordrecht Confession

Dutch Anabaptists set their beliefs down in eighteen articles. The document outlasts them, shaping Mennonite and Amish faith for the next four centuries.

1660

The Martyrs Mirror

Thieleman van Braght gathers the stories of the persecuted into one enormous book. In plain homes it becomes second only to the Bible, a record of what faithfulness cost.
